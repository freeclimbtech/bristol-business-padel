How it works

Why padel works for business networking

It's not just that padel is having a moment. The format itself does something most networking events can't.

Padel is always doubles. There's no singles game, which means the moment you step on court you're paired up, usually with someone you don't know well yet. Compare that to a normal networking event, where you can spend twenty minutes hovering near the coffee before anyone talks to you. On a padel court, that problem doesn't exist. You're on a team inside the first minute, whether you like it or not.

The court itself helps too. Padel is played in a smaller, enclosed space than tennis, with glass walls that keep the ball in play. Rallies last longer, the ball moves slower, and there's far less time spent jogging off to retrieve it. Practically, that means more time standing next to your playing partner between points, which is exactly when the actual conversation happens. Nobody's business gets discussed mid-rally. It happens in the gaps, and padel gives you a lot more gaps than most sports do.

It's also genuinely easy to pick up. Half the group at any given Business Padel Networking session hadn't played before their first Friday. That matters, because a lot of sport-based networking quietly gatekeeps by ability, five-a-side or squash can be miserable if you're paired with someone far better or worse than you. Padel's forgiving pace and smaller court mean a complete beginner and a regular can still have a genuinely competitive, enjoyable game. Coaching helps too. Every session has proper instruction on hand, so nobody's left standing at the back not knowing what to do.

Then there's timing. Two hours is long enough to actually get to know someone, not just exchange a business card, but short enough to fit before a working day starts. Starting at 8am and finishing by 10 means you've had a proper morning before most people have opened their inbox. And because the group moves straight from court to breakfast, the conversation that started over a shared game keeps going over coffee, which is where most of the actual networking happens.

None of that is an accident. It's why we built the session around it: coached, all abilities, two hours on court, breakfast after. If you haven't tried it yet, Friday's a good place to start.
